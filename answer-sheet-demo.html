Section 3 — Reading Comprehension (Q17–24)
Passage: Urban farming has emerged as a viable solution to food insecurity in densely populated cities. By converting rooftops, vacant lots, and even indoor spaces into productive growing areas, municipalities can reduce their dependence on long supply chains. Studies suggest that a single hectare of urban farmland can supply fresh produce to approximately 200 families annually. Furthermore, urban agriculture contributes to biodiversity, reduces heat islands, and improves community well-being. Critics, however, point out that the initial infrastructure costs remain prohibitively high for low-income communities, and that water usage can offset some environmental gains. Despite these challenges, pilot programmes in cities such as Singapore, Detroit, and Taipei have demonstrated measurable improvements in both food access and neighbourhood cohesion.
